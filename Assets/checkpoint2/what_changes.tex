\begin{callout}{Bottom line}
The instructor's verdict is right and the fix is smaller than it looks. Keep the
interest (an agent that hands a decision back to a person), keep the
within-subjects machinery, the replayed traces, the FictionalQA filter and the
MiPP anchor, and drop what could not be run: the expert population, the
learning post-test, the invented corpus, and the ``escalates vs.\ never
escalates'' manipulation. What replaces them is Option~1: fix the escalation
points, vary the \emph{form} of the escalation (modal stop, inline flag,
provenance-only) within subjects, on a frozen snapshot of the CSU CS systems
documentation, with students new to that system. The escalation points are
\emph{context-dependent forks}: two pages give different answers, both correct
for someone, and which applies depends on a fact about the participant that
the assistant was never told. On each trial the assistant streams a short cited
procedure and pre-fills a card of dropdowns; the participant may check the
pages, then submits. Whether they checked, whether they kept or changed the
agent's default when it was right and wrong, and what it cost are what is
measured. The redesigned proposal is \texttt{new\_proposal.pdf} (about five
pages including the mock-ups); this memo explains every change and what you
still have to do by hand.
\end{callout}

\section{Why the ``newer page wins'' rule was dropped (new in v3)}
\begin{wwhtable}
Replace date conflicts with context-dependent forks. &
Version 2 keyed every conflict on ``last updated'' dates. Coen identified the
flaw and it is real: (1) a competent agent could resolve a date conflict
itself, so escalating it is bad agent design and a reviewer would say ``fix
the agent'' (MiPP and Mishra justify handing a decision to a human only when
the system cannot resolve it); (2) it turned verification into a mechanical
instruction-following task (compare two dates); (3) it did not fit the RQ,
which is about when a human is actually needed. &
Escalate only forks the documents cannot settle alone: two pages give
different answers, each correct for someone, and which applies depends on a
fact about the user the question does not state. Example: GPU Jobs lists RTX
3090s (24\,GB) on \texttt{kestrel-gpu} and A100s (40/80\,GB) on
\texttt{peregrine-gpu}; ``set up a GPU job for my PyTorch model'' does not say
how much memory the model needs. \\
\rowsep
Split the task card: ``Your situation'' / ``What you asked''. &
The deciding fact must exist for the participant but not for the assistant,
and people ask short questions. &
\emph{Your situation}: one or two lines only the participant sees (``your
model needs about 30\,GB''). \emph{What you asked the assistant}: the short
question, the only input the assistant receives. \\
\rowsep
Keys from page scope plus situation; no dates, no authority rule, no rule
card. &
The key must be derivable from the frozen snapshot alone (no time or money
for administrator verification) and must not depend on a stated rule. &
Each page states its own scope (memory per GPU; ``off campus, use the VPN'';
``not backed up, scrubbed''). Key $=$ page scope $\times$ situation line.
Default $=$ the higher-ranked retrieval option. Default-correct items reuse the
same pages with a different situation line (a model that fits in 16\,GB). \\
\rowsep
Escalation names the options, not their conditions. &
If the escalation said ``24\,GB vs.\ 80\,GB'', nobody would need to open a
page and verification would be undefined. &
``Two pages give different GPU options: kestrel-gpu (RTX 3090) or
peregrine-gpu (A100). I'll use kestrel-gpu.'' Deciding requires opening a page
and checking it against one's situation; in provenance-only, opening a page is
the only way to notice at all. \\
\rowsep
Say why the agent does not ask up front. &
A reviewer will ask why the agent does not collect the user's situation
first. &
One sentence: the agent cannot know which user facts matter until retrieval
reveals the fork mid-task; hence mid-task escalation. \\
\rowsep
Sharpen the C5 tension. &
MiPP's C5 says stay silent or downgrade when uncertain about the
\emph{user's context}, which is exactly this case. &
Stated in Related Work: here silence means quietly applying a default that is
wrong for this user; provenance-only is C5 taken literally, and the study
tests it. \\
\end{wwhtable}

\section{Study design}

\textit{Note added 10 October 2026.} The paper draft (\texttt{Assets/paper/main.tex}) supersedes the design below on one point: task set is no longer tied to escalation form, nor arranged in a $3\times3$ Latin square of set and form. Each block holds three tasks, one from each set, so set is crossed with form; the pool holds nine forks, three per set, each in two twin versions, and the assignment of forks to forms and twins to participants rotates with the participant number (\texttt{system/escalate/rotation.py}). The target is $N=12$, two participants per block order, giving 108 measured tasks.

\begin{wwhtable}
Review-and-submit trial with a dropdown-only run card. &
Reproducing a procedure is slow and hard to score; typed answers need
adjudication; the earlier draft left the participant's task undefined. &
Task card $\rightarrow$ assistant streams 3--4 cited steps (about 3\,s each)
$\rightarrow$ run card of 3--4 \emph{dropdowns} (3--4 options each, drawn
from the corpus) pre-selected with the assistant's choices $\rightarrow$ submit.
60--90\,s per item; participants never type; scoring is automatic (scored
field $=$ key); nothing is adjudicated. \\
\rowsep
Score one field, hide which one; do not ask ``is the assistant correct?''. &
A card that asked only for the forked value, or a correctness question,
would announce the fork in every condition and erase the manipulation;
people act on output rather than grade it. &
The scored field's options include both diverging options plus 1--2
plausible alternatives; the other fields are correct, uncontested decoys.
Control items cite two agreeing pages so two chips never signal a fork and
give a baseline page-opening rate. \\
\rowsep
Define the forms by two properties, not by MiPP's families. &
In the earlier draft the quiet form still had a ``sources differ'' badge. &
Announced? Blocking? Modal: yes/yes (stream pauses, overlay, card disabled
until a choice). Inline: yes/no (same sentence on the fork step, card
pre-filled, ignorable). Provenance-only: no/no (plain step with two citation
chips). Same steps, default, pages, controls and undo in all three. Figure~1
of the proposal shows all three for item G1. \\
\rowsep
6 items per block; three hypotheses; trimmed measures. &
21 items did not fit an hour; five hypotheses and a dozen measures at
$N{=}12$--18 reads as fishing. &
4 flagged (2 default-correct, 2 default-wrong) $+$ 2 control per block;
2-item practice block; about 45 min. H1 verification, H2 appropriate reliance
(form $\times$ default-correctness), H3 cost. Measures: verified, scored-field
decision, time, raw TLX, one intrusion item, manipulation check, ranking with
reason. Cut: need for cognition, other MiPP-Eval items, dwell thresholds. \\
\rowsep
Stay within subjects; handle carry-over explicitly. &
Between subjects needs about three times the participants (4--6 per
condition at class scale). &
Each critical fact in one item only; task sets from disjoint sections; tell
everyone up front the assistant can be wrong; all six orders; position
covariate; first-block descriptive check. \\
\rowsep
Frozen JSON item records; written difficulty matching. &
Participants must see identical content; the Latin square assumes
exchangeable task sets. &
Record $=$ situation, question, steps with citations, fork step index,
options, default, card fields with options and pre-fill, scored field, key.
Match sets on steps, card fields, question--page overlap band, fork type,
fork position; pilot; item random effect. \\
\rowsep
Power in one sentence. &
The power table cost a quarter page and said one thing. &
``A paired $t$-test at $N{=}12$ detects only large participant-level effects
($d_z \approx 0.9$), which is why trial-level models are primary; H2 power is
simulated from the pilot.'' \\
\end{wwhtable}

\section{The agentic claim}
\begin{answerbox}{Direct answer: does this need agentic RAG, and is extending MiPP necessary?}
\textbf{Strictly, no, the question does not require an agentic system.} It
requires a system-initiated, mid-task escalation with provenance and a
fallible default; any RAG assistant with a fork detector that fires while
producing a multi-step procedure satisfies that, and the outcomes are human
behaviours that do not depend on the pipeline behind the trigger.

\textbf{What ``agentic'' buys is a real decision point that a chatbot cannot
produce.} A chatbot answers the question as asked. Only a loop that retrieves
per step and inspects its own retrieval can discover mid-task that the answer
depends on a fact the user did not state, and choose to hand the decision back.
That is the course's ``if a chatbot can do it'' test, passed without agency
being the IV, and it is stronger in v3 than in v2: escalating a date conflict
was something the agent should have resolved itself, whereas escalating a
context-dependent fork is the one case where handing back is correct. The word
stays honest only while four things hold: the task is multi-step; the
escalation is agent-initiated at the affected step; the trigger is the
pipeline's own recorded decision (the fork check: retrieval-score margin plus
an LLM judge asked whether the chunks give different answers depending on a
user attribute the question does not state), not a hand-seeded flag; and the
loop is described in one figure and one paragraph. If any fails, say ``a RAG
assistant with system-initiated escalation.''

\textbf{Extending MiPP is the frame, not the paper.} Use MiPP for three things
only: the constraints held constant across forms (C1 brevity, C2 one-step undo,
C3 provenance); the C5 tension that provenance-only tests; and the
perceived-intrusion item for comparability. The forms are not MiPP's families
and are not claimed to be. The theory for the DVs comes from the CHI/CSCW/IUI
reliance literature (Bu\c{c}inca, Bansal, Vasconcelos, Schemmer) and McFarlane
for interruption. Never write ``first test of MiPP''.
\end{answerbox}

\end{itemize}
