### Contamination (QA) filter
- Generation: up to 2 question/answer pairs per chunk, each tied to an evidence span; a candidate is rejected if the evidence is not verbatim in the chunk, the answer is not in the passage, the answer exceeds 12 words, or a field is empty. `qa.py`, `config.json` (`qa.per_chunk` 2).

### Retrieval and fork check (task builder, `system/escalate/tasks.py`)
- k = 4. `config.json` (`retrieval.k`), `MODEL_VERSIONS.md`.
- Margin = top-1 minus top-2 cosine score over the whole index for the scripted query; threshold 0.10 ("margin_small" when margin <= 0.10). `tasks.py`, `config.json` (`agent.fork_margin`).
- Judge: `fork_check()` prompt asks whether the passages give DIFFERENT correct answers for the step depending on a fact about the user the request does not state; returns `depends_on_user`, `attribute`, and options (value, chunk_id, applies_when). Options are kept only if grounded (cited chunk is among the passages and the value appears in it verbatim or with >= 60 % of tokens). "judge_yes" requires `depends_on_user` and >= 2 distinct grounded values. `agent.py` (`fork_check`, `grounded_options`), `tasks.py`.
- The judge in the task builder sees the top-k chunks plus the two authored option passages (deduplicated, in rank order) at a fixed step "Extract the answer to the question from the documentation." (no planner). `tasks.py` (`_evaluate`).
- Fired = judge_yes AND margin_small. Fired on the authored fork = fired AND >= 2 authored options matched by the judge's grounded options (token overlap >= 0.5 against the option's value, condition, or highlight). `tasks.py`.
- Query selection: the scripted query and then each `alt_queries` phrasing are evaluated in order; every evaluation is logged; the first phrasing that fires on the authored fork becomes the task's scripted query. `tasks.py`. Attempts needed: J1 4, S1 2, all other selected forks 1. `system/data/tasks/*.json` (`fork_check.query_attempts`).
- Log: `system/data/fork_check_log.jsonl` holds 25 evaluations over 15 candidate forks. Of 25 queries: judge_yes 19, margin_small 23, fired 17, fired on the authored fork 8. Forks with a query that fired on the authored fork: 8 of 15 (J1, J2, R3, R4, R6, S1, S2, S3); forks with any firing: 12 of 15. Counted from `fork_check_log.jsonl`; same 17/8 figures in `RUNBOOK_CHECK.md` and `task_catalog.md`.
- Recommendation = the option whose passage ranks higher for the scripted query (tie within one chunk: earlier in the text); that passage is Source A. Source B is the other option's passage, or, if both options sit in one chunk, the catalogue's `rule_source`/`secondary_source` passage (else the next distinct hit). `tasks.py`.
- Task-level blind probe: one probe question per fork; the model answers blind and informed (with the two source passages); "survives" = blind wrong AND informed right. 13 of 15 forks survived; J3 and R2 did not. Counted from `fork_check_log.jsonl` (`probe.survives`); same in `task_catalog.md`.

### Participants
[N] students at Colorado State University who reported no prior use of the department's Falcon cluster took part ([n] women, [n] men; ages [range]; [n] upper-division undergraduates and [n] graduate students). They were recruited through [the course's data-collection form] and [were not compensated]. [Three] pilot participants were run before data collection and are not included.

### Apparatus

**Corpus snapshot.** The corpus was a frozen snapshot of the CSU Computer Science department's systems documentation (sna.cs.colostate.edu), crawled on 3 October 2026 by a breadth-first crawler with a 0.5 s delay between requests: 77 pages (23,965 words of extracted text), stored as raw HTML and as light Markdown with each page's title, URL section, "Updated on" date and SHA-256 hashes recorded in a manifest. Pages were split at headings into 226 chunks of at most 180 words per packed paragraph group (fragments under 25 words dropped) and embedded with nomic-embed-text (768 dimensions; cosine similarity on normalised vectors). The snapshot, chunks, index and every model output were committed so the pipeline replays byte-identically.

**Drafting pipeline and fork check.** All assistant behaviour was generated before the study with a local open-weight model (qwen2.5:7b-instruct, Q4_K_M, via Ollama 0.35.1; temperature 0, top-k 1, seed 567) and frozen as JSON records; no model ran during a session. For each candidate fork the pipeline retrieved the top k = 4 chunks for a scripted query and ran the fork check, which fired when the cosine margin between the top two chunks was at most 0.10 and an LLM judge, shown the retrieved passages, answered that they gave different correct answers depending on a fact about the user the query did not state, with at least two options grounded verbatim in the passages; a fork was used only if the judge's grounded options matched the two authored options. The option whose passage ranked higher became the assistant's recommendation and that passage was Source A; the other option's passage (or, when both options sat in one passage, the page stating the governing rule) was Source B. Over 25 query phrasings for 15 candidate forks the check fired on 17 and on the authored fork on 8 (hit rate 32 %); each fork's deciding fact was further screened with the blind-versus-informed check of Kirchenbauer et al. [ref], in which the same model answered a probe question without and with the two source passages, and 13 of 15 forks were answered wrongly blind and correctly informed.

**Study interface.** The study ran as a single-page web application (plain JavaScript served by a local Python HTTP server) in a desktop browser. The left pane held the block's spec sheet, the task question, one answer field and Accept and Reject buttons; the right pane held the scripted query, a Send button and the assistant's trace. After Send, seven trace lines appeared one at a time behind a typing indicator, each after a fixed delay of 3.0–5.0 s stored in the task record (derived from a hash of the task id and line number), so every participant saw identical content and timing; lines 3 and 4 named Source A and Source B as chips that opened the frozen page in an overlay over the trace with the cited lines highlighted. Line 6, the fork line, was the only line rendered differently by form. Under S1 the trace paused and a modal ("Which option should this task use? Source A suggests [option].") listed both options as buttons while the answer field stayed empty until a pick; under S2 the line "Use [option] (Source A)" carried a warning glyph and the same recommendation sentence beneath it and the field was filled with the recommended option; under S3 the line read "Use [option] (Source A)" alone and the field was filled with the recommended option. In every form line 7 reported the decision and filled the field, Accept ended the task, Reject opened a two-option picker whose choice refilled the field, and Reject could be pressed again before Accept. The chips remained clickable in every form, including while the S1 modal was up, and no feedback on correctness was shown.

**Logging.** Every interaction was written to a local SQLite database under a participant code: block start and end, task start, Send, each trace-line onset, fork onset, modal pick, each source open and close with its duration, autofill, Reject, picker choice, refill and Accept, each with the elapsed time since task start and a UTC timestamp. For each task a summary row recorded the final value, accuracy (final value equals the key), followed (final value equals the recommended option), verified (a source viewed for at least 3 s of summed dwell before Accept), source pattern (recommended source only, non-recommended only, both, neither), dwell time and number of openings per source, decision time (fork-line onset to Accept) and total time (task start to Accept).

### Materials and tasks
Participants completed 12 measured tasks in three sets of four plus two practice tasks. Each set was built from two context-dependent forks: two documented options whose correct choice depended on one fact that the scripted query omitted and that appeared only on the block's spec sheet. Each fork was used twice with the same query, trace, sources and recommendation; the two twins differed only in the sheet line and the task question, so that under one line the recommendation was correct and under the other wrong, giving two correct and two wrong recommendations in every block. Set 1 (job submission; assignment "Fine-tune an image model on Falcon in stages") contained the QoS fork (gpu_short vs. gpu_medium, decided by wall time) and the GPU-partition fork (kestrel-gpu vs. peregrine-gpu, decided by GPU memory and GPU count); Set 2 (access and remote connection) the Jupyter fork (plain jupyter-notebook vs. jupyter-notebook --no-browser with an SSH tunnel, decided by whether the user sat at the machine or connected over SSH) and the credentials fork (CS Linux account vs. CSU eName@colostate.edu, decided by the service being signed into); Set 3 (storage and software) the output-location fork (home directory vs. /s/<hostname>/a/tmp, decided by output size and whether it had to be kept) and the shell start-up fork (modules.sh vs. modules.csh, decided by the login shell). Each spec sheet listed the four deciding facts, one per task, under the block's assignment heading and stayed on screen for the block. Recommendation correctness was never set by editing the agent: the recommended option was always the higher-ranked retrieval option, and the key for each task was the option the documentation gave for its sheet line, the other option being either invalid under that line (e.g., a 30 h job exceeds gpu_short's 24 h limit) or scoped by the page to the other situation. The two practice tasks had no fork; their traces cited two agreeing sources ("Both sources agree: ...") and filled the field.

### Design
The experiment was a 3 × 2 within-subjects design with factors Escalation Form (S1 modal stop, S2 inline warning, S3 provenance-only) and Recommendation Correctness (correct, wrong). Each form was administered in one block of four tasks drawn from a fixed task set tied to that form (Set 1 with S1, Set 2 with S2, Set 3 with S3), with two tasks per correctness level per block presented in a pseudo-random order seeded by participant code; block order was counterbalanced with the six permutations of the three forms, [n] participants per order. Dependent variables per task were verification (any source opened), source pattern and dwell per source, following (final value equals the recommendation), accuracy and decision time; [per block, raw NASA-TLX, a perceived-intrusion item and a manipulation check; per session, a ranking of the forms with a reason] [only if these instruments are built before the study]. With [N] participants this gave [12N] measured tasks, [2N] per cell.

### Procedure
Sessions took about [45] minutes. After [consent and] instructions stating that the assistant was usually right but could be wrong, that the spec sheet described their assignment and that they could open any source the assistant cited, participants completed the two practice tasks, during which the experimenter had them open and close a source and use Reject and Accept once each. They then completed three blocks of four tasks, one block per form; at the start of each block the experimenter selected the form and its task set, and the block's spec sheet replaced the previous one and remained on screen. On each task the participant pressed Send, watched the trace, could open Source A or Source B, and pressed Accept or, after Reject and a choice from the picker, Accept; no feedback on correctness was given. [Between blocks participants completed the NASA-TLX, intrusion and manipulation-check screens, and the session ended with the ranking questionnaire and a debrief.]
